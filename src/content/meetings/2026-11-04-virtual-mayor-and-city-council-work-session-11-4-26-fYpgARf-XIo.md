## Discussion by agenda item

### Agenda item: Staggers Road improvements bid
What happened: Director Miller requested award of Contract LA 26-007 for Staggers Road. He described a 2-inch mill and overlay, heavy-duty pavement fabric at the Marshall Avenue intersection for SAIA trucking traffic, targeted sidewalk and curb and gutter replacement, reconstruction of non-compliant ADA ramps, bikeways in both directions consistent with the 2026 bikeway master plan, new solar-powered lighting where there is none now, and high-visibility markings and crosswalks. He noted the corridor is near the former bowling alley where a Mega Mart grocery store is going in. He said seven bids were opened October 14, 2026, and recommended the low bidder, SFMS LLC of Gaithersburg, a certified minority business enterprise, at $279,155.99 plus a 7.4% contingency of $20,730.99, for a total authorization of $299,886.98, funded through the FY2027 CIP. He said the contingency is below the usual 10% because that is all that remains in the budget.

Councilmember Clark asked whether the city has a recommended route for large trucks. Miller said trucks coming down Route 216 from Howard County are forced onto local streets, that he had met with Delegate Lehman [name uncertain] about signage before the county line, and that State Highway said it would add signage this fall but had not answered a follow-up. He said sidewalks will be moved away from large trees and kept on the current side to avoid stormwater management issues. Councilmember Simmons thanked staff for downward-facing lighting. Councilmember Mills said the road has long been rough; Miller said he submitted a list of six or seven inlets needing state attention.

Resident relevance: If built, the project adds ADA ramps, bikeways, crosswalks, and lighting on a corridor staff described as pedestrian-heavy.

Outcome / next step: Moved to the November 9, 2026 meeting.

### Agenda item: Police interview room systems bid
What happened: Cornwell-Shiel recommended a new five-year Axon Interview Room contract with Axon Enterprise of Scottsdale, Arizona, for $65,417.40, replacing all hardware in the police department's three interview rooms with five years of support, warranty, and unlimited evidence hosting. He said three bids were solicited through the Sourcewell contract: Axon $65,417.40, Motorola Solutions $61,720, and Creekside Consulting (Case Cracker) about $61,599.15 as a five-year equivalent of a three-year quote. He said Axon was chosen for features, mainly integration with evidence.com. First-year funding of $13,083.48 is in the FY2027 IT operating budget; later years will go into future budget requests.

Outcome / next step: Moved to the November 9, 2026 meeting.

### Agenda item: Resolution No. 7-2026 youth leadership council
What happened: Councilmember Clark introduced Resolution No. 7-2026 to create a Laurel Youth Leadership Council of seven members 18 or younger, appointed by the Mayor and confirmed by Council, serving one-year terms, with two adult advisors, to advise on matters affecting children, youth, and families. She said it does not duplicate the Youth Services Commission. She proposed two amendments: make one seat at-large for a student who attends a Laurel school, and set meetings at least quarterly. Councilmember Kole asked about staffing and cost; Clark said no budget would be assigned and no additional cost was expected, and that some committees, including the tree board, may be combined. President Smith, Councilmember Simmons, and Councilmember Mills expressed support; Mills and Simmons suggested youth civics education ideas.

Outcome / next step: Moved to the November 9, 2026 meeting. President Smith asked Clark to submit her verbal amendments in writing.

Timestamp: [00:24:34]

### Agenda item: Resolution No. 8-2026 animal welfare committee
What happened: Councilmember Clark introduced Resolution No. 8-2026 to rename the No Kill Community Animal Shelter Citizens Advisory Committee as the Animal Welfare Citizens Advisory Committee and shift its purpose from planning one facility to advising on animal welfare citywide. She said it keeps current members and terms and commits no city funds. She recounted that dissolving the committee was proposed at the July 1 work session. Mayor Sydnor said his office never told the committee to stop meeting and that the administration supports the resolution. Councilmember Mills called the rename unnecessary, said the city should find space for a no-kill shelter, and suggested a site off Lafayette. The Mayor said zoning would have to change first, that Prince George's County is responsible for animal control, and that city animal control holds animals 24 hours or less before taking them to the county. Councilmember Simmons and President Smith supported the resolution.

Outcome / next step: Moved to the November 9, 2026 meeting.

### Agenda item: Charter Resolution No. 184 budget preparation
What happened: Councilmember Simmons introduced Charter Resolution No. 184, amending Article 500, Section 502 (budget preparation). He said the section was last revised in 2000, the budget is about $80 million, and Council now has about 18 business days to review it. As drafted, it codifies an initial budget vote in the final week of May and requires the budget 45 days earlier; he said the June 30 adoption deadline in Section 503 still applies. President Smith said Council already has 61 days from May 1 to June 30. Councilmember Kole said Council reviews rather than builds the budget and asked whether a draft could come earlier; City Administrator Pulley said departmental printouts of projected FY28 numbers would come earlier and the budget before May 1. In response to Kole's question to Ms. Anderson, the respondent said a fixed date is easier to advise on but is not a legal issue. Councilmember Clark asked whether the 61 days include weekends (Director Saylor confirmed they do) and urged scheduling the vote only after the full period. Saylor said a numbers printout could be ready around the middle to third week of April, ideally April 15. Councilmember Mills said nothing needed to change if a draft is provided.

Simmons said he would revise the resolution so a draft budget with annotations arrives at the 45-day mark (about April 15) and the full proposed budget on May 1; Smith and Mills said they could agree to an April 15 draft date.

Resident relevance: If adopted, residents would have a set date when draft budget figures reach Council.

Outcome / next step: Simmons to modify the resolution. President Smith moved it to the November 9, 2026 meeting.
