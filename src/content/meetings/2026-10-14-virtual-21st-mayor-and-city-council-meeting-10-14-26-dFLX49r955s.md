## Discussion by agenda item

### Agenda item: Composting update and trash collection rules
What happened: Tim Miller (Director of Public Works) and Michelle Blair [name uncertain], introduced as assistant sustainability manager, presented composting results since mandatory composting began in July 2025. Miller said the pilot averaged 7 to 8 tons per month and the program now averages about 21 tons. He reported September participation by route: Monday 16.1%, Tuesday 12.4%, Wednesday 18.8%, Thursday 11.4%, and Friday (Old Town and Dorset Road) 20.3%, with most participating households composting at least twice in the four monitored weeks. He said composting saves $65 a ton compared with trash, saved nearly $16,000 in tipping fees from July 2025 to June 2026, and could save about $81,000 a year at 75% participation and over $107,000 at 100%. In answer to Councilmember Mills, he said Brown Station charges $90 per ton for trash, the MES composting facility $25, and recycling $27.

Blair described outreach: the Green Living web page, a public dashboard, a refrigerator magnet with a QR code, neighborhood report cards (Thursday route graded A and Monday route D for diversion rate), crew photos through Vision software, and tagging followed by letter notices.

Councilmember Mills said Ward 2 participation was low and asked for more education there; he also suggested residents use paper leaf bags instead of plastic bags and floated removing trash cans to push recycling. Mayor Sydnor asked about bags left outside cans; Miller confirmed that as of October 14 bags are no longer picked up, that the 65-gallon can is emptied while an extra bag beside it is tagged and left, and that one truck is implementing tagging. Miller said larger cans are not provided without an audit with Blair. Blair said residents may have more than one recycling and compost bin, and that compost may go in the tan bin loose. Council President Smith asked that residents be told they can request extra bins.

Resident relevance: If you put bags beside your city trash can, they may be tagged and left; extra recycling and compost bins are available on request.

Outcome / next step: No action was taken. Blair said she will host an America Recycles Day event in the council chambers in November. Miller announced an electronics, Styrofoam, and paper shredding event that Saturday at the DPW yard, 305-307 First Street, before noon.

### Agenda item: Employee health, dental, and vision insurance contract
What happened: Lisa Woods [name uncertain], Deputy Director of Budget and Personnel Services, presented a September 18, 2026 memo recommending Cigna for employee health, dental, and vision coverage for November 1, 2026 through October 31, 2027. Resident relevance: Saylor and Kole said the flat rate means no change in the city's premium share paid with public funds.

### Agenda item: Charter Resolution No. 183 budget adoption date
What happened: Director Saylor introduced Charter Resolution No. 183, amending Article 500, Section 503 of the City Charter to change the date by which Council must pass the annual operating and capital budget from June 30 to June 20. She said a 2025 state law (Tax-Property Article 6-303) requires municipalities to set the property tax rate before June 20. President Smith opened and closed the first public hearing at 6:59 p.m. with no one signed up. Councilmember Simmons said the resolution moves the whole budget rather than only the tax rate and said he leaned toward amending it. Councilmember Clark said she supports his recommendation.
